EV charger installation

A home charge point is a dedicated circuit from your consumer unit to a wall-mounted unit, with its own protection and earthing arrangement. The install itself is straightforward. What matters is whether your incoming supply can take it — and that is the part that gets skipped.

The load assessment nobody mentions

A 7.4kW charger draws 32A continuously, often for hours. Add an electric shower, an oven and a hob and a house on an older supply can exceed what the main fuse is rated for. The regulations require a proper maximum-demand assessment before installing, and where the supply is tight the answer is a load-managed charger that backs off when the rest of the house is drawing heavily, rather than an upgraded supply costing hundreds.

I do that assessment before quoting and tell you which of the three situations you are in: fine as is, needs load management, or genuinely needs the DNO to upgrade the supply.

Earthing — the reason some quotes are cheaper

Most houses round here are on a PME (TN-C-S) earthing system, which is not permitted for outdoor charging without protection against the supply's neutral going open-circuit. That is handled either by a charger with built-in PEN-fault detection or by an earth rod. A quote that does not mention earthing at all has not considered it.

Common questions

Can I get a grant?

The main domestic grant closed to most homeowners in 2022. The EV chargepoint grant is still open to people in flats and rented accommodation, and there is a separate scheme for landlords. I will tell you honestly whether you qualify rather than quote as though you do.

How long does installation take?

Most installs are half a day to a day, depending on how far the charger is from the consumer unit and whether the cable run is straightforward.

Do I need my supply upgraded?

Usually not. Most houses take a 7.4kW charger fine, and where they do not, a load-managed unit is nearly always cheaper than a supply upgrade. I assess it properly before quoting rather than after starting.

Can you fit a charger on a shared drive or a flat?

Sometimes, but it depends on cable routing, ownership of the parking space and permission from the freeholder or management company. Worth a conversation before you buy a unit.
